Left: mean labelled vessel width at the labelled centreline divided by the FOV diameter (×1000), one dot per image, bar = dataset mean. Relative to eye size, FIVES vessels are drawn 2.6× as wide as HRF's (6.08 vs 2.30). In pixels the picture differs: FIVES 12.3 px, HRF 7.8, DRIVE 2.5, because pixel width also follows image resolution and eye size in pixels (542 px in DRIVE, 3376 px in HRF); only the relative width reflects the labelling convention.
Right: width_ratio = predicted / labelled vessel width, measured at the labelled centreline points the model found (so it compares widths of vessels the model did detect, not missed vessels). Marker = 3-seed mean of the dataset mean, bar = range over seeds. Settings as in the Labelling-style test section of REPORT.md (label_style_test.py): frozen models, no training, no TTA, threshold 0.5, 3 seeds; LODO models on their own held-out dataset, models trained on all 113 pooled images on FIVES (792 images).
The thinning is shared: width_ratio is 1.02–1.04 on LODO (mean of the four datasets) and 0.68–0.72 on FIVES for every variant, learned or not. It does not separate the models: hybrid's extra FIVES loss against rgb is missed vessels (centreline recall -0.056), not thinner ones (width_ratio -0.009).
Variants shown: rgb, clahe, hybrid, enh3, hybrid_weber.
